## 2. LEP's observed significance versus a conditional sensitivity forecast

In [the 2003 LEP paper](https://drive.google.com/file/d/18WZPXj8kdapzRLemh9jaJlSE2u9RW8P7/view), Fig. 7/related text at approximately pp. 69–71, at an SM Higgs test mass 115 GeV/c²:
- **Observed:** `1 - CL_b ≈ 0.09` for the observed final event configuration; paper reports approximately **1.7 standard deviations** from the background hypothesis.
- **Hypothetical signal-world median:** `1 - CL_b ≈ 0.009` when a **115 GeV Higgs is assumed to exist**, by its distribution's median expectation. This is neither an observed p-value nor a certificate of discovery. It is also **not** the 'significance that would have been obtained had LEP continued in 2001'.
- The **September 2000** `1 in 1,000` figure from Abbott's report cannot be directly pooled or temporally identified with either 2003 final quantity without matching event selection, test-mass search, calibration, nuisance assumptions, global/local significance conventions and analysis version.

The paper explicitly describes experiment-level heterogeneity (ALEPH initial excess; L3/OPAL mild preference; DELPHI deficit) and final detector/beam calibration and sometimes revised selection/analysis procedures (Introduction). Therefore, **acquisition ending in 2000 did not freeze inference**, and comparing p-values does not identify causal contributions of particular post-shutdown analysis changes.

**Correction to overstrong previous wording:** The final LEP text alone does **not** prove why the provisional significance changed by any unique statistical contribution; it establishes that updated calibrations/analyses were included and reports final combined results. A quantitative decomposition requires the actual prior event/likelihood inputs and version-specific systematic assumptions.

## 3. Two rival meanings of opportunity cost — not a new EPISTEME theory

[Florio & Pancotti](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1IOpH6cYeDhZA5vTEYK_C4CVml-xIdIj_/view) explicitly treat the 27-km former LEP tunnel used for LHC as an existing asset: the *historical construction cost* should not be counted as new LHC investment cost merely because LHC uses it (sunk-cost accounting). Separately they discuss **shadow prices and opportunity costs of employed resources**.

Thus **`sunk historical tunnel cost = 0 in incremental new-facility assessment` does not imply `marginal opportunity cost of keeping LEP operating = 0`**. Further LEP occupancy could consume installation schedule, human resources, budget or safety/engineering slack; those quantities need historically sourced estimates. Such distinctions are established economics, not a discovery.

Abbott's September contemporary report of CHF 7m for a one-month extension with no claimed LHC delay provides a *different intervention* from the requested much longer 2001 running proposal. Neither implies an identified optimum.

## 4. A simple observational-equivalence counterexample (analysis only)

Let A=1 denote a long LEP extension granted; the observed action was A=0. Let Q denote the contemporaneous expected scientific benefit, C the contemporaneous marginal opportunity cost, and G an independent decision-level exclusion gate. We do **not** claim these were measured actual CERN internal variables.

- **Model M1 (ordinary resource choice):** `A = 1{Q > C}`; choose `Q=0.4, C=0.8`. Then observed `A=0`.
- **Model M2 (gate constraint):** `A = (1-G) * 1{Q > C}`; choose `Q=0.8, C=0.2, G=1`. Then observed `A=0`.

Both models can be made consistent with the same observed nonextension, the same LEP archives and the same later 2003 likelihood summary. But `do(G=0)` changes A in M2 and there is no corresponding gate effect in M1. **The choice outcome plus post-decision statistical evidence cannot identify whether an independent gate suppressed an otherwise favorable research choice.**

This is a deliberately minimal textbook-type causal-identification counterexample, **not** a new theorem, a validated historical model, or proof that a gate existed. To discriminate these explanations, require historically sourced ex-ante Q/C estimates, feasible alternative schedules and provenance-backed committee constraints or votes. A public institution's post-hoc statement alone is not a complete internal preference measurement.

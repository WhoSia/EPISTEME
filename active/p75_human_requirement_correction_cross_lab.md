## II. Why repeatedly prescribing humans was a scientific error

1. **Estimand category error.** Whether a token is present in a historical Wikipedia revision, a source-relative referent binding is valid, a formally stated proposition follows from declared premises, or a decision under explicit costs dominates is not in itself a claim about a *new human reader's preference*.
2. **Authority laundering through reviewers.** Two raters can agree and share an error; a crowd cannot independently certify the original source bytes, historical access time or semantic external truth. [MQR 4.104](https://app.notion.com/p/3f4ef561cf9281dbacb3df7dbc8dbd55) exposes this directly. Human count is not independent witness proof.
3. **False dependency gate.** Old P75-P0 `independent_reviewers >= 2` made every model-conditional SWITCH impossible absent humans. This was an author-selected **design choice**, not a theorem about epistemic legitimacy.
4. **Synthetic-count trap.** Producing 48 hypothetical reviewer slots instead of proving the correct source-linked witness and cutting alternate derivations made progress look busy but did not answer the founding question.
5. **Opposite ambition trap.** Clean certificate machinery also must not crowd out actual discovery. The answer is not more synthetic gates; it is source-confirmed mismatch mechanisms that defeat strong rivals on *existing source and real transition decisions*.

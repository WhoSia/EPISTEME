## JADE: positive witness for recoverability, not irrevocable loss
Bethke and Wagner, *The JADE experiment at the PETRA e+e− collider: history, achievements and revival*, EPJ H 47 (2022) article 16, https://doi.org/10.1140/epjh/s13129-022-00047-8 , openly records that original data were saved in Exabyte archives and moved to modern storage in December 2005. A small set of luminosity files (per-run integrated luminosities, needed for cross sections) was missing from routine digital preservation. A paper printout was found by Jan Olsson at DESY; because the green faded paper was not scannable/OCR-readable, values were manually typed. Five typing errors were found and corrected using a **preserved software checksum routine**. The source explicitly documents a successful *hybrid physical-paper plus computational* restoration, not an impossible-to-recover deficit. Original detector simulation/reconstruction also revived.

Science positive outcome: Bethke et al., *Determination of the strong coupling αS from hadronic event shapes ... using JADE data*, EPJ C 64 (2009), https://doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s10052-009-1149-1, observed running coupling behavior matching asymptotic freedom and excluding no running at 99% confidence under that paper's analysis choices. Pahl et al., *Study of moments of event shapes ...*, EPJ C 60 (2009), https://doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s10052-009-0930-5, measures αS at 14–44 GeV with specified systematic components. **Do not infer either specific event-shape result individually requires the recovered per-run luminosity files**: event-shape distributions and cross-section-normalized outcomes have different denominators. Need inspect original analysis scripts and whether luminosity actually enters the exact chosen estimand. Proposed *future source-authorized* query: one published luminosity-dependent physical cross section / its uncertainty reconstruction, not αS as a convenient substitute.

This case directly challenges the claim that an archive's digital omission implies permanent defeat-route extinction.

## ALEPH: named science queries with explicit exclusion, unusually strong negative candidate
ALEPH Collaboration, *Statement on the use of Aleph data for long-term analyses*, approved 4 December 2003, https://dphep.web.cern.ch/sites/default/files/archive_data.pdf, says archived data, final MC and software are preserved but **cannot repeat all analyses**, especially those needing systematic-effect information. Explicit examples: Z lineshape, W mass, tau polarization, forward/backward asymmetries, many heavy flavor results, Higgs and SUSY searches. The declaration is contemporaneous authorized *policy/source testimony*, not an independently executed negative replay proving fundamental impossibility or irretrievability. It differentiates specific estimands and available evidence; find which exact condition/calibration covariance is absent from public retained archive and whether later analyses overcame it.

The signed/dated provenance matters: this pre-dates some later modern preservation systems. Follow-up needed on policy supersession and ability today, rather than carry 2003 judgments unchanged into 2026.

## DELPHI: preserved data/software, explicit community limitations
DELPHI Collaboration 12 March 2024, CERN Open Data DOI 10.7483/OPENDATA.DELPHI.0X8O.FBUH: https://opendata.cern.ch/record/417 and https://dphep.web.cern.ch/delphi-preservation-policy/ . SHORT DST and RAW are archived, data on CERN EOS backed up by CTA, software released via CERN GitLab/CVMFS and adapted to modern architectures with most functions verified. The collaboration warns high-precision replication is discouraged because tacit detector/run-conditions expertise is fading. **Counterbalance:** software is not simply lost; the important residual may be qualified analysis authority, uncertain nuisance/systematic modelling or missing tacit skill. No blanket executable impossibility assertion.

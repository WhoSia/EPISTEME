## 1. Unit of analysis: avoid a three-level equivocation

A candidate case comprises the same scientific domain, a specific pair or family of competing representations/standards and a dated decision horizon. Four **different** outcomes must be independently observed.

1. **Individual conversion affordance:** marginal cost to translate or execute a researcher's existing task using a second representation, accounting for interface errors and validation work (not only token/time cost).
2. **Collective adoption/coordination:** share of researchers/facilities using each system and whether a switch is individually, collectively or institutionally feasible. Observe converter ownership/pricing to test Liu et al. (2011).
3. **Executable tests:** independent distinguishable experiments satisfying technical and resource/permission constraints, recorded with source, instrument, computational requirements and realistic scheduled capacity.
4. **Evidential warrant:** likelihood/reliability of evidence and whether the experimental community accepts the method for the hypothesis. Must avoid simply equating official recognition with scientific truth or assuming a new format loses epistemic value.

A claim about level 4 cannot be inferred from level-1 switching cost alone, nor can a change of equilibrium at level 2 prove that level-3 scientific testing possibilities actually disappeared.

## 3. Minimum evidence table for candidate historical/technical case

| Field | Required evidence | Unacceptable shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Research problem | Exact empirical target, rival hypotheses and predicted divergent observation | 'better science' as a vague claim |
| Data source/date | original paper, source byte identity/version, temporal ordering | later analysis treated as ex-ante actor information |
| Translator intervention | exact adapter/version/cost/accuracy; exposed and hidden validation | just saying 'AI made it easy' |
| Individual response | per-user switch propensity, skills, error correction and opportunity cost | individual survey alone as community adoption |
| Aggregate outcome | actual standard distribution and converter provider incentives | inference from price or profitability to epistemic lock-in |
| Test availability | time-indexed scheduled instrument capacity, permissions, computational budget | paper-only hypothetical test judged executable |
| Scientific admissibility | specific historical reliability/acceptance criteria, alternative evidence routes | treating recognition as truth or lack thereof as data failure |
| Competitor rejection | matched parameter/constraint/test, outcome not covered by stronger comparator | changing rival's assumptions arbitrarily until it fails |
| Postdecision robustness | compare original versus revised data/calibration without hindsight leakage | attributing all revised significance to institutional choice |

## 4. Abstention and termination rules

- If an enlargement of individual options is accompanied only by a decline in observed adoption, presume **existing equilibrium/coordination mechanisms** before asserting epistemic novelty.
- If changes to scientific tests are merely changes in resource allocation or field standards already treated in research literature, call it existing mechanism, even when historically interesting.
- If an effect only exists in an invented toy model, classify as illustrative/pilot, not scientific discovery.
- If the documentary record does not separate causal hypotheses, explicitly mark **NOT IDENTIFIED**. Do not create false calibration parameters to imply a positive result.
- If no distinguishable scientific-warrant mechanism survives and the research questions are subsumed by MQR, recommend transfer/terminal review; never auto-close without explicit user authority.
- The 12-week research residency is capped and can stop sooner. **No P78 or PAPER promotion.**

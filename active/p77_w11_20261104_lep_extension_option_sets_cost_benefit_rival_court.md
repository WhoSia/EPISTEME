## 1. Research question and adverse result

Does the decision to end LEP data acquisition in November 2000 exhibit an independent historical mechanism of *foreclosed scientific criticism*, or can the observed choice be explained by pre-existing research-infrastructure opportunity cost and decision theory?

**Verdict at W11:** The strongest defensible result is a *time-indexed comparison of feasible option sets*, not a new lock-in or epistemic-loss theorem. CERN extended LEP on 14 September 2000 to 2 November **on the expressed premise that the LHC construction schedule would not be affected**, then declined a substantially different **2001** extension on 8 November citing interference with LHC construction. These are not obviously inconsistent institutional preferences: the marginal feasibility and opportunity costs differ. No independent causal effect or irrational decision is established.

## 3. Testable reconstruction without hindsight leakage

Let the **choice** at time t be a_t in the institutionally and physically feasible option set F_t. An ex-ante decision representation is

U_t(a)=E[V_{LEP}(a)+V_{LHC}(a)+V_{other}(a) | I_t] - E[C(a)|I_t],

where I_t is the information available **at t** and the expectation includes uncertainties about discovery prospects, delay, personnel, safety and engineering constraints. This is deliberately a generic decision-theoretic comparator, **not an identified historical utility function**.

**Key counterexample to simplistic lock-in:** the September short extension is a revealed *local option opening* under publicly stated non-disruption; the 2001 extension is an apparently materially different action with longer occupation of a shared facility. Hence F_September and F_November need not have the same actions/costs. The September extension is direct counterevidence to any categorical claim that CERN had an inflexible no-extension preference.

**Not identifiable with present evidence:** probability assigned to the ~115 GeV signal at the time; joint cost/delay distribution; scientific valuation of foregone LHC time versus gained LEP information; feasibility of alternative installation schedules; the counterfactual result of running LEP during 2001. Do not choose numerical values to force an institutional-closure narrative.

# EPISTEME-P69 — Paper Manuscript Blueprint (NOT a submitted paper)

**Working title:** Task-Conditional Representation Effects in Evidence-Based LLM Evaluation: Robust Sign Reversals, Scoring Artifacts, and the Limits of Cross-Task Transport

**Target audience:** empirical NLP evaluation / robust measurement / knowledge-intensive LLM evaluation. No venue acceptance, novelty certification or empirical P69 model results is asserted.

## Abstract skeleton — factual portion only
A series of preregistered EPISTEME tests previously observed task-conditional response patterns under controlled evidence-representation operators. P56 reported a high task×representation descriptive variance share in one three-task stopping-time surface; P59 observed 16 operator-effect sign reversals under its fixed comparison rules while its 15 adjusted prospective tests did not reject. Subsequent attempts to explain the effects by a record/state carrier-framing hypothesis (P62) and a matched structural evidence-location hypothesis (P65) failed their specific predeclared transport courts. This archive motivates, but does not establish, a generalizable non-transport claim. P69 constructs a packet-only answer-and-minimal-rationale evaluator, truthful retention controls and independently sourced SciFact evidence ingestion to scrutinize format selection, scoring artifacts, transfer and task ecology validity. No P69 provider experiments or independent human evaluations have yet been run, so no novel confirmatory model outcome can be reported.

## Research questions
- RQ1 (measurement): How much of historical operator sensitivity survives a semantically informed, evidence-grounded scorer and explicit provider-format nonresponse accounting?
- RQ2 (task transport): Can a representation-effect signature estimated in other families improve out-of-family prediction beyond matched held-out task-only anchors?
- RQ3 (replication): Are R/T effect directions coherent across A/B invocation blocks, separate output contracts and a second distinct model/provider family?
- RQ4 (external ecology): Do *source-annotated* scientific claim/evidence archives exhibit a comparable problem after independently blinded human truth auditing, or do synthetic grammar artifacts explain the appearance?

## Ruling on target inference
The current eight tasks represent only **four** authored mechanism clusters. Four signed-symmetry labels admit a smallest two-sided reference tail of 0.125; 8,192 repeated invocation outcomes are not 8,192 independent task-mechanism samples. No valid claim of a universal non-transport theorem, a generic causal mediation, or a population 5% result can follow from the present observational design alone. A larger independently authored ecology, with honest clustered uncertainty, is indispensable before strong paper confirmation. The independent SciFact dataset is one source ecology, not 16 new mechanism families.

## P69 prelaunch experiment ordering
**Gate A**: independent masked human judgment of 72 synthetic case examples and SciFact original-versus-matched external evidence cases; unresolved or low-agreement items remain HOLD, never posthoc fixed by oracle labels.

**Gate B**: ex-ante selection of at least six genuinely distinct task ecologies *if using a six-cluster all-signs-agree two-sided reference*; the number six establishes finite p-value resolution only, not sufficient power or representativeness. Preserve original four synthetic families as mechanism-specific blocks.

**Gate C**: provider-specific API canaries proving supported output contracts and versioned model IDs; P67 format-pilot results or equivalent explicitly validated evidence before P69 pilot.

**Gate D**: frozen main inference and alternative reporting branches before expensive calls. Precommitted 192-call instrumentation ceiling and 8,192-call main ceiling cannot be treated as automatic authorization.

**Gate E**: only after Gates A–D, model-family panel and held-out negative-controls evidence; stop/modify scientific claim if scorer normalization erases sensitivity or external task-family predictions succeed.

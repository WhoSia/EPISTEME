## Surviving research question
Can a *prospectively specified* semantic-equivalent editing operation plus a source-evidence context predict the sign (and interval) of a scientifically consequential adjudication error on genuinely held-out tasks, beyond generic prompt sensitivity and scoring artifacts? Infer bounds on the family of tasks for which such transport is warranted.

## Defining estimands
Let t=scientific task, s=source/evidence scenario, m=model, e∈{original, edited} and y∈{0,1} independently judged evidence-warrant correctness. Let p_m(t,s,e)=Pr(y=1). The context-conditioned average paired effect is Δ_s=E_{t,m}[p_m(t,s,edited)-p_m(t,s,original)]. Strong sign reversal requires Δ_given and Δ_challenge of opposite signs *and* uncertainty intervals excluding zero, not simply a significant interaction. Primary prospective endpoint should be correct warrant decision with ground truth derived from independently curated source records; mention accuracy may be a secondary endpoint. Crucial null: Δ_s≈0 after adjudication-invariant scoring.

## Main validity hazards
1. Counterfactual item semantic equivalence must be verified by blinded humans in complete context; W9 first/second mention contexts have leakage/cue concerns and require revision before reader testing.
2. Judging model may be sensitive to the same treatment; rubric-based independent human review plus scoring blind and calibration required.
3. Task selection and editing operator chosen on P57–P69 outputs risk selection bias; prospectively seal genuinely unseen tasks.
4. Carrier×role and domain changes may conflate task difficulty. Matched item pairs and repeated runs, seed/order/random effects necessary.
5. Scientific evidence correctness must be tested against source-grounded propositions rather than an answer key written to favor the expected sign.
6. External novelty beyond cited prompt robustness must be proved by a pre-registered hard rival predicting equally well.

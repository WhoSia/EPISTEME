## IV. Modest monotonicity lemma — important but mathematically trivial, NOT a novelty claim

Let there be n researchers. Their individual action sets before/after conversion are A_i and B_i with A_i subseteq B_i, and define T(a_1,...,a_n) as the set of scientific tests possible under the *same fixed* collective-resource and admissibility constraints with joint action a.

Define the **physically/institutionally attainable** test sets:

R(A)=union over a in product_i A_i of T(a),  
R(B)=union over b in product_i B_i of T(b).

Then because product_i A_i is a subset of product_i B_i and T is unchanged, **R(A) is a subset of R(B)**. Proof: each old feasible joint profile remains a profile after the expansion, with the same attainable tests.

**Meaning and scope:** In a fixed world with literally expanded action sets and unchanged joint feasibility, the *existence of a possible coordinated testing configuration* cannot shrink. This does **not** imply decentralized equilibrium choices, observed test frequency, cost, institutional acceptance, reliability, epistemic warrant or overall research welfare improve. An apparent counterexample must violate/relax at least one premise (endogenous resources, incentive/equilibrium selection, coordination, institution, changed outcome map) or switch from possibility-set to realized-selection outcomes. Do not put forward a tautological union-inclusion as new formal philosophy.

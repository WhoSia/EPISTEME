## 2. Critical physical-historical reading

### Abbott (2000) — contemporary perspective vs hindsight
Abbott's *Nature* report, dated 21 September 2000, describes the one-month LEP extension, an estimated **CHF 7 million** incremental cost rearranged within existing CERN budget, and Maiani's statement that LHC timing and cost would not be disrupted. The report discusses five candidate events and a then-reported ~1-in-1000 random-pattern probability. **Do not equate this September number with the later November LEP-wide 2.9-sigma provisional combination or with the 2003 final significance.** It also states corroboration across four detectors/decay modes was still required. This is reporting of then-current expectations, not a verified independent calculation.

### LEP Working Group (2003) — actual retrospective evidence re-analysis
The final paper combined **2461 inverse picobarns** across ALEPH, DELPHI, L3 and OPAL at centre-of-mass energies 189–209 GeV. A 95% confidence lower bound on SM Higgs mass of **114.4 GeV/c²** was published. At 115 GeV/c², the final combined background tail probability was **1-CL_b ≈ 0.09 (~1.7 sigma)**. The introduction explicitly reports **ALEPH excess, slight signal preferences in L3/OPAL, and a DELPHI deficit**, not four uniformly positive independent detectors. It explicitly attributes final analyses to final beam/detector calibrations and sometimes revised analytical procedures. Thus acquisition closure did NOT freeze evidential judgement, but altered calibration/analysis is not evidence of fresh collisions.

The likelihood method uses binned signal/background terms by reconstructed mass and discriminant; statistical uncertainty, correlated systematic variation, smoothings and interpolations matter. A chronological comparison of significance cannot by itself causally decompose the effects of calibration, selection, model updates and the original data without analysis versions.

### Mattig & Stöltzner (2019) — independent philosophical prior art, and temporal scope
Original full journal paper reports 2011/2012 questionnaires and interviews before and after **2012 LHC Higgs discovery**, framing the discovery as a qualified 'crucial experiment', addressing Duhemian underdetermination by accumulated reliable particle-physics practices, and contrasting epistemic and pragmatic model-choice values. It explicitly rebuts simplistic stories about uniformly strong pre-discovery certainty. **Its fieldwork is not a contemporaneous witness to the 2000 LEPC decision.** It does defeat an EPISTEME claim that 'the dual role of epistemic and pragmatic values in particle-physics theory choice' is novel.

### Florio & Pancotti (2020) — economic rival; exact claim is narrower than retrospective exaggeration
This encyclopedia article explicitly formulates a social cost-benefit framework with research use-value, non-use value including **quasi-option value**, capital/operation costs and discounting. The full text directly uses the LEP/LHC 27-km tunnel reuse as a **sunk-cost accounting** example: do not count the original tunnel construction cost again as a cost incurred by LHC. This is not proof of what the 2000 CERN management's private LEP extension decision model contained. It strongly defeats generic originality claims about opportunity costs, social benefits and alternative research infrastructures; it does not itself adjudicate the historical 2000 meeting.

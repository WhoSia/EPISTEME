## Abstract

A scientific community may persist with an inferior theory not only because replacing it is expensive, but because the means of testing the alternative are themselves dependent on the history of the incumbent research infrastructure. The distinction is intuitive, yet none of its broad versions should be treated as novel: theories shape experiments, networks affect technology adoption, options are costly to maintain, and archives can lose the software and expertise needed for reanalysis. The question left for EPISTEME is narrower and causal. Under what conditions does the prior adoption of a representation or measurement system change the feasible set of *future rival-discriminating tests*, even when the rival remains logically falsifiable? We formulate a minimal state-dependent distinction among data access, executable verification and warranted scientific discrimination. We give an observationally indistinguishable-world construction illustrating why apparent absence of contrary evidence does not identify absence of a superior alternative. This construction alone proves no new mechanism. We then specify the counterfactual and documentary evidence necessary to distinguish path-dependent loss from ordinary missingness, resource constraints and existing provenance accounts. Positive restoration evidence from JADE and documented constraints from ALEPH and DELPHI help discipline this test without yet satisfying it.

## 1. What the project originally asked

The founding question of EPISTEME in September 2026 was not whether modern science uses restrictive data formats. It was whether networked notation, software, measurement devices, institutional standards and emerging translation systems change a community's ability to *discover, demonstrate and adopt* better scientific alternatives.

An archive can make inquiry portable. A software standard can allow new investigators to revisit old results. A translated representation may lower switching costs. But these gains do not imply that every discriminating measurement is still feasible, or that records retain the assumptions under which a conclusion was defended. The same infrastructure can act as memory, migration scaffold and barrier.

An early proposed 'translation-layer paradox'—that interoperability may also amplify lock-in—was reduced by mature converter and standards-competition theory. The broad claim that scientists' existing beliefs shape evidence production was also anticipated by social epistemology and theory-motivated experiment models. Such prior-art defeats are not incidental setbacks. They prevent us from using an appealing story as a contribution.

## 2. From willingness to test to capacity to test

Let S_t be the incumbent epistemic infrastructure and H_t the historical sequence of investments, adoption decisions, training and retired tools. Let A(S_t,H_t) denote the set of scientifically feasible verification procedures at time t, conditional on cost and authority constraints. For candidate rival R and incumbent I, a procedure a is *discriminating* when its outcome distribution, under an explicitly specified measurement model, differs enough between R and I to bear on their adjudication.

A rival can be logically falsifiable even if no discriminating procedure a currently belongs to A(S_t,H_t). This distinction between test willingness and test capability is meaningful; it is not automatically new. Experimental design, research-option maintenance and path-dependent infrastructure already discuss its components.

The research burden is to find whether adoption history is an independent explanatory cause of changes in A, rather than merely correlated with funding, technological obsolescence, record loss or an investigator's choice not to run a test.

## 3. What absence of defeating evidence cannot establish

Imagine two histories that leave an identical present collection of accepted publications, retained datasets and institutional software. In one history, a rival had no genuine empirical advantage under feasible tests. In the second, an advantage existed but a now-retired measurement regime was the only one that could discriminate it. The surviving records alone do not distinguish those histories. This *observational equivalence* is an identifiability warning, not proof that the second history occurred.

More strongly, even the second history would not establish that adoption of the incumbent theory caused the retirement. The instrument may have disappeared because funding ended, the experimental site closed, or superior alternatives emerged. The causally relevant variable must be isolated. An attractive counterfactual does not count as an observed one.

## 4. Reduction court: what is already known

Network effects and standards can impede adoption; converters may ease or intensify lock-in. Science-of-science work models theory-shaped experiments, dissent suppression and self-confirming learning. Research-option models allow costly maintenance and irreversible discarding of alternatives. Data-preservation and provenance scholarship distinguishes bytes from instruments, calibration, executability and expertise. Therefore none of these broad propositions can be assigned to EPISTEME as original findings.

The residual hypothesis is more exacting: a historical shift in the installed epistemic infrastructure caused a change in the availability of a *particular, scientifically warranted rival-discriminating experiment*, and this change influenced a subsequent scientific judgment or feasible judgment. Evidence must defeat competing explanations from conservation budgets, software availability, general archive decay and deliberate scientific prioritization.

## 5. Three discriminating historical cases

JADE supplies a strong restoration counterexample: missing machine-readable luminosity information was reconstructed from paper with a surviving integrity-checking routine. Its lesson is that local digital absence need not equal irreversible loss. No scientifically warranted paper should infer that the restored luminosities caused the later event-shape alpha_s findings until the relevant processing chain is traced.

ALEPH's 2003 archival policy names analyses for which surviving files and software were regarded as insufficient, including Z-lineshape and W-mass precision measurements. This is targeted historical testimony, not a present-day proof of impossibility.

DELPHI's 2024 policy points to a different barrier: available data and adapted software coexist with diminishing detector/run-condition knowledge and a caution against high-precision replication. This suggests why an executable procedure need not settle whether the resulting inference is scientifically warranted.

The cases establish candidate *mechanism components*. They do not yet reveal a compelling counterfactual adoption-induced irreversible loss, nor a documented superior scientific rival that became untestable because of that adoption.

## 6. An empirical program that could fail

First, freeze a scientific rival pair and one measurement capable of discriminating between them, rather than selecting a convenient metric after studying the archive. Second, establish whether the original apparatus, calibration protocol, software, training and records enabled that measurement. Third, document a specific infrastructure adoption event. Fourth, evaluate what remained executable and scientifically warranted afterward. Fifth, compare contemporaneous alternative paths, successful preservation programs and natural controls. Finally, test whether the lost capability materially changed a historical or prospective rival adjudication.

The hypothesis should be rejected or substantially narrowed if an independent route reliably reconstructs the evidence, the allegedly missing knowledge never existed, the decisive scientific query is unsupported, or standard preservation/prior-art models already predict the full outcome.

## 7. Conclusion

The ambition of EPISTEME is neither a denunciation of standards nor a metaphor about scientific stagnation. It is to understand whether some histories of inquiry remove the very experiments through which their alternative histories could have been justified. The available evidence warrants a research program and bounded negative cases, not yet a novel general law. The distinction between a preserved proposition and a preserved ability to challenge it remains the question to solve.

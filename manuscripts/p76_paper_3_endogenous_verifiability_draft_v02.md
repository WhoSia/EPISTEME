# Paper 3 — When the Infrastructure of Inquiry Determines Which Alternatives Can Be Tested: An Adversarial Model with Reconstruction Controls

**Writing Loop W4 · v0.2 · 2026-10-20 · theory research draft / causal claim unproved / PAPER HOLD.**

## Abstract

Historical scientific infrastructure conditions what can be recorded and recomputed, but that observation alone does not show that the adoption of an incumbent system irreversibly suppressed testing of a superior rival. We distinguish this stronger causal claim from four established mechanisms: information missing from an archive, nonexecuting legacy code, untrusted uncertainty models and missing expert interpretation. A dated ALEPH preservation policy provides a purported case of limited precision-measurement replay; a 2026 independent reconstruction project presents a strong and informative adversarial check. The project reports a partially recovered hadronic Z lineshape, revisions to source luminosities and event-scan grouping, and a non-circular repair of a 32% excess in a preliminary pole cross-section estimate. It also reports a roughly 3.6σ width discrepancy and restricted scan-curvature identification; its derived neutrino and coupling quantities require external inputs. The coexistence of substantial numerical restoration with explicit limitations shows why 'data preserved' and 'original measurement revalidated' are distinct. It does not establish path-dependent theory defeat or an EPISTEME-original principle. We formulate a falsifiable historical adoption-effect estimand and outline controls that could distinguish endogenous loss of discrimination from ordinary sample limitation, retirement or incomplete calibration.

## 1. What would count as the original claim?

EPISTEME's founding question asks whether an installed epistemic infrastructure can reshape the future feasible set of experiments by which a superior rival might defeat an incumbent. Prior-art defeats matter: classic standards, information-cascade, option-maintenance and data-preservation work already treats coordination, information and the maintenance of scientific capability. Relabelling the inability to reproduce a dataset analysis as 'defeat-route extinction' is not a new contribution.

Let H denote historical adoption history, X available equipment and resources, and q a specified analysis that discriminates rivals I and R under an independently defended measurement model. Define C(q;H,X) as the set of procedures available to reproduce or extend q with authentic source evidence and uncertainty. The *prospective hypothesis* is that two feasible historical regimes with distinct H but adequately controlled X can have different C(q;H,X), and that this change measurably altered actual rival adjudication. This is an estimand requiring historical comparison, not something demonstrated by definition.

## 2. Strong rival world: identical archives without adoption-induced loss

Consider two worlds whose present public archives have similar event files and incomplete calibration records. In World A, a community's installed-standard transition caused a specialized analysis route to be retired. In World B, an unrelated funding withdrawal and normal detector retirement removed the same tool and expertise, while the representation standard had no causal role. Both histories can yield the same missing procedure today. An archive-only audit cannot identify the causal arrow. Even showing a modern fit fails is insufficient to distinguish them. This construction is elementary, and its point is methodological restraint, not theorem novelty.

A strong observational research design requires detailed adoption timelines, resource and personnel controls, a physical question whose testing depended on the retired process, and a comparison case in which the capability survived or was restored.

## 3. ALEPH supplies an adversarial control, not a settled loss witness

ALEPH's official statement (2003) listed Z lineshape and W-mass precision analyses among archival tasks it could not simply repeat, particularly because systematic-error documentation was insufficient. A later public reconstruction project provides a partial counterpressure. Its own log describes archived hadronic-skim events, published luminosities, a 1994 full-simulation anchor, ISR-convolved fitting and an externally supplemented set of electroweak estimates. It records a correction from an overly high preliminary σ⁰ estimate due to an extra acceptance factor, and corrections of mismatched scan luminosities and scan-period grouping.

This sequence establishes that the absence of full original precision reproducibility is not the same as no available scientific computation. The project reports M_Z 91.1638±0.040 GeV, Γ_Z 2.4508±0.012 GeV and σ⁰_had 42.47±0.69 nb. It also acknowledges Γ_Z at around −3.6σ relative to its PDG target, a sensitivity of roughly −430 MeV when the 1993 peak scan anchor is excluded, and derived quantities dependent on external leptonic inputs. We independently read repository records but have **not independently rerun the fit or validated detector-systematic covariance**. Therefore neither 'precision reconstruction succeeded' nor 'all archived verification routes are forever lost' is established by this case.

The philosophical inference is accordingly negative and stringent: archived values + executable model + numerical convergence do not automatically supply measurement authority. A separate authoritative error and calibration chain is needed. This is already a theme of scientific-data preservation literature, not an EPISTEME-original law.

## 4. Where an adoption effect might still be found

Four ingredients must be jointly authenticated:
1. A contemporaneous institutional adoption or retirement decision concerning a measurement infrastructure, with credible chronology.
2. A specified rival-scientific pair and an analysis that *could previously discriminate* them, not a convenient retrospective statistic.
3. A documented change in test feasibility or scientific warrant after adoption, beyond normal sample or funding shifts.
4. An alternative source, comparison institution or recovery intervention that supplies leverage on what would have happened without that adoption.

The rival explanation is not only 'someone forgot to preserve code'. It can be that the later scientific question was never identifiable from the original design, the retained sample is unrepresentative, the uncertainty model always relied on informal expertise, or a more useful measurement displaced an obsolete one. Any of these could falsify the distinctive adoption claim.

## 5. Historical tests and explicit failure criteria

JADE exemplifies partial restoration when an independent physical record and an old checking program survive. ALEPH shows that a historical exclusion policy can coexist with later computational reconstruction attempts, making a binary irreversibility story unsuitable. DELPHI retains substantial source data, conditions records and adapted programs despite warnings about high-precision expertise. All are better used as *contrasting controls* than as three confirmations of one mechanism.

P76 would fail to isolate the proposed adoption effect if every observed contrast can be explained by DPHEP's existing preservation levels, ordinary archive completeness, budgetary change, sample selection, or expert availability. P77 should open only if a distinct independently source-authorized mechanism or rival-discriminating world observation emerges beyond these descriptions.

## 6. Research-to-writing discipline

Manuscript 1 may establish source-level case narratives; Manuscript 2 may establish technical equivalence of one NeXus source pair; neither manuscript may donate an unproved causal inference to this third paper. Writing Loop drafts disclose missing evidence rather than filling it with a fluent explanation. The presence of a publicly posted independent ALEPH analysis should invite a reproducibility audit, not automatically reverse an archival policy or certify the original measurement.

## Conclusion

What inquiry preserves is not captured by the existence of a file, a fit, or even a modern software environment. Yet a theory of endogenous scientific defeat routes needs more than this insight. It needs a documented historical choice that altered available discriminatory tests and a convincing account of what rival theories would have predicted instead. Until such evidence exists, EPISTEME's founding proposition remains a constrained research question.
